## What does payroll compliance mean for your organisation?

A completed pay run answers one question: did the process finish? A compliance review asks a different question: were the relevant obligations met, and can you show how you reached that conclusion?

This guide is for HR and Finance leaders with payroll accountability and Payroll teams checking their own processes. It provides a practical review framework, not an exhaustive statement of Australian employment law or a certification checklist. Confirm the workplace relations system and instruments that apply to your organisation and obtain advice where coverage or interpretation is uncertain.

Use three questions to organise the work:

- **What should happen?** Establish the applicable obligation and its effective date.
- **What actually happened?** Trace source data through calculation, payment and reporting.
- **What supports the conclusion?** Retain the evidence, unresolved exceptions and review decision.

## Start with the applicable obligations

Build an obligation map before testing calculations. Record the employee group, applicable instrument, classification decision, relevant pay rules and the person responsible for keeping the assessment current.

Fair Work explains that [modern awards](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/employment-conditions/awards/about-awards) set minimum pay and conditions for covered employees. Coverage and classification need careful assessment; a job title alone is not a sufficient working basis for a payroll rule.

Your assessment may need to consider legislation, an enterprise agreement, an award and contractual commitments. Document how the relevant sources interact rather than assuming one document resolves every entitlement. Our guide to [modern award interpretation mistakes](/blog/modern-award-interpretation-mistakes) explores that part of the review in more detail.

A useful obligation record includes the source, version or effective date, interpretation or advice relied on, affected employees and corresponding system rule. Keep unanswered questions visible instead of silently converting assumptions into configuration.

## Rebuild the review scope from previous payroll incidents

If your organisation experienced underpayments and overpayments in 2025, the starting point is the incident history, not a blank checklist. Separate confirmed findings, suspected causes, completed corrections and fixes that have not yet been tested. Assess whether incorrect pay is continuing while the historical work proceeds.

Build a coverage map across legal entities, employee groups, instruments and their effective dates, pay codes, locations, systems and review periods. Include acquired businesses, legacy configurations and employees who moved between agreements or systems. An issue affecting one group is a reason to test comparable groups, not proof that they have the same problem.

**Illustrative enterprise scenario—not a client case study:** a business with two enterprise agreements and an acquired payroll system found missed shift allowances and duplicate retrospective adjustments in 2025. Some employees were underpaid and others overpaid. Payments were corrected, but managers still submit late changes and the interface still permits a file to be reprocessed.

The next review should trace the allowance rule and effective dates, reconcile missing inputs, identify duplicate adjustment identifiers and examine which other groups use the same interfaces. Keep each finding and its affected population separate. The companion [governance guide](/blog/payroll-governance#resolve-cross-functional-disputes-with-a-decision-record) follows the same scenario through ownership and implementation.

## Check the inputs before relying on the output

A calculation can be internally consistent and still produce the wrong result if the inputs are incomplete. Reconcile the population and source records before interpreting the results. A walkthrough can explain a process; it cannot establish the accuracy of every employee payment.

### Employee and employment data

Check that changes to employment status, classification, work location and agreed arrangements reach the right systems with the correct effective date. Reconcile approved HR changes with payroll updates; an approval email does not establish that the update was implemented.

### Hours, leave and variable payments

Trace worked time, leave, allowances and other relevant inputs through the interfaces and approval process. Look for rejected files, missing approvals and manual workarounds. Ask how an item arriving after cutoff is identified, assessed and resolved.

### Configuration and change testing

Document why a rule is configured as it is and test scenarios that challenge it. Include boundary cases relevant to your workforce, such as a changed classification partway through a pay period or a shift attracting multiple conditions. Retain the expected result, actual result, reviewer and resolution of any difference.

These are suggested controls. Select and adapt them to the risks in your organisation; they are not a prescribed audit methodology.

## Review payments, pay slips and records

Fair Work's [paying wages guidance](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/pay-and-wages/paying-wages) explains minimum obligations concerning payment. Include the timing and method of payment in your assessment rather than examining calculation accuracy alone.

Its [record-keeping and pay slips fact sheet](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/tools-and-resources/fact-sheets/rights-and-obligations/record-keeping-pay-slips) covers accurate and complete employment records, including for salaried employees. Check the applicable requirements against the records you actually hold and the pay slips employees receive.

As a practical assurance exercise, select a completed transaction and ask another reviewer to reconstruct it from the retained evidence. Can they explain the inputs, applicable rule, calculation, approval and payment without relying on the original operator's memory? Document gaps and decide whether they affect other transactions.

## Include tax, superannuation and reporting

Employment entitlement checks do not replace tax and superannuation checks. Give these workstreams explicit owners and confirm the current rules and effective dates relevant to the payment being reviewed.

The ATO describes [Single Touch Payroll](https://softwaredevelopers.ato.gov.au/STP) as reporting salaries and wages, PAYG withholding and super information through payroll software. Treat reporting acceptance as evidence about the submission; it does not independently establish that all underlying employee entitlements or payments were correct.

Use the ATO's current [employer STP guidance](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/hiring-and-paying-your-workers/single-touch-payroll) and [super for employers guidance](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/super-for-employers) when confirming requirements. Assess other relevant obligations, including state or territory payroll tax, with the appropriate adviser and revenue authority.

For historical corrections, distinguish the original entitlement period, payment date and reporting treatment. Refer technical questions for advice rather than assuming that current settings apply unchanged to every earlier period.

## Handle overpayments separately from underpayments

First establish whether a variance is a genuine overpayment, an entitlement interpretation issue or a timing difference. Preserve the original transaction, calculation and relevant employee records. Track underpayments and overpayments separately so that aggregate totals do not hide who was affected.

Fair Work explains that deductions to recover overpayments are permitted only in limited circumstances. It recommends discussing repayment with the employee and recording an agreed arrangement in writing; if agreement cannot be reached, seek legal advice. Do not assume written agreement alone makes every proposed deduction lawful. See [Fair Work's overpayments guidance](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/pay-and-wages/deductions-and-related-issues/overpayments).

**Recovery is different from offsetting.** A genuine mistaken payment is not the same as an above-minimum salary amount that an employer seeks to apply against an entitlement. Fair Work's [guidance following the September 2025 Coles and Woolworths decisions](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/newsroom/news/offsetting-and-record-keeping-salaried-employees) says excess amounts in one pay period generally cannot satisfy a shortfall in another under contractual offsetting arrangements. It also distinguishes those arrangements from award or agreement annualised wage provisions. Obtain advice on the arrangement and methodology rather than automatically netting all differences across a year.

Give employees a clear explanation and a way to query the assessment. Record disputed amounts and agreed next steps separately from completed payments or recoveries. Have the relevant advisers confirm tax, reporting and superannuation treatment for corrections, particularly across financial years. A recovery decision does not close the underlying process defect.

## Demonstrate that a fix keeps working

Agree closure evidence before implementation. For a significant finding, the pack should identify the cause, affected scope, approved change, test results, unresolved limitations and the reviewer’s conclusion.

Re-test the original failure scenario, related scenarios and cases that should remain unchanged. For the illustrative business, that means testing missing allowance inputs, replayed adjustment files and legitimate new adjustments—not simply checking that the interface runs.

Then monitor operation across an agreed number of relevant pay cycles, chosen for risk and workforce patterns. Include infrequent events such as termination or retrospective changes through additional testing where they do not occur during observation. A clean fortnight does not prove that an annual or occasional process works.

Keep historical correction, current-pay containment and permanent control improvement as separate completion decisions. Reopen the finding if the error recurs or new evidence expands its scope.

## Monitor changes and recurring warning signs

Build a review schedule around your workforce and changes to its obligations, data and systems. Review triggers can include a new agreement, pay-rule change, system migration, acquisition or repeated employee questions about the same issue.

For example, three similar employee queries may indicate a shared input problem. Record the pattern, test that explanation and assess the potentially affected population. Do not label it systemic before investigating, but do not close the matter merely because the three payments were corrected.

Our [payroll risk versus payroll error guide](/blog/payroll-risk-vs-payroll-error) explains how to distinguish an observed incident from continuing exposure. Track both the immediate correction and the action intended to prevent recurrence.

## Prepare for a payroll compliance review

An organised evidence pack helps a reviewer understand the scope and limitations of the work. Assemble:

- The employee population, review period and systems in scope.
- Relevant instruments, classification assessments and documented interpretations.
- Source data and an explanation of missing records or known data limitations.
- Configuration records, change history and test results.
- Pay, payment and reporting reconciliations relevant to the scope.
- Previous findings, employee queries and the status of corrective actions.

Agree the review question before requesting extracts. A configuration review, sample-based process assessment and employee-level calculation comparison answer different questions. State what the work can establish, what it excludes and how exceptions will be investigated.

## What if you identify a possible underpayment?

Preserve the evidence and assign a response owner. Assess whether the issue is still affecting current pay, investigate its cause and determine the population and period potentially affected. Obtain appropriate specialist advice on the response and applicable obligations.

The [Fair Work payroll remediation program guide](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-04/Payroll%20remediation%20program%20guide.pdf) provides guidance for employers addressing underpayments. Our [payroll remediation guide](/blog/payroll-remediation) explains the programme journey, while the [remediation checklist](/blog/payroll-remediation/checklist) provides an editable action tracker.

Keep the correction and the underlying fix as separate decisions. A payment may resolve an identified shortfall while a data, policy, process or configuration issue remains open. Rebecca Wade's approach connects the findings to operational action: identify the driver, agree who will resolve it and test whether the fix works.
