## Use the checklist in a joint discussion with Payroll

Choose a recent anonymised change, approval or issue. Ask the responsible people to explain what they would decide, what evidence they would request and where they would seek help. Record an action where the answer depends on an undocumented assumption.

This is a role-specific learning aid. For broader learning pathways, read the [payroll training guide](/blog/payroll-governance-training-guide). To record observations and development actions, use the [capability assessment worksheet](/blog/payroll-governance-capability-uplift).

The allocations below are discussion prompts, not a prescribed structure or a statement of personal legal liability. Confirm actual delegations and specialist responsibilities within your organisation.

## HR checklist: make employment decisions usable by Payroll

An approved employment decision still needs a complete and traceable payroll handoff. Work through these five questions with the people preparing and authorising changes.

<tbody>
<tr><th scope="row">1. Can we explain the basis for this employment change?</th><td>Relevant facts, approved decision, effective date and any advice relied on</td><td>Identify the authorised decision-maker or specialist before turning an assumption into an instruction</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">2. Does Payroll have the information needed to implement it?</th><td>Complete change instruction and confirmation of receipt</td><td>Resolve missing facts with the source owner; record any timing implications</td></tr>

For classification and instrument questions, use the [award-interpretation guide](/blog/modern-award-interpretation-mistakes) to frame the enquiry. A leader does not need to improvise a technical answer to demonstrate accountability.

## Finance checklist: understand what the approval evidence establishes

Ask what each reconciliation or report proves and what remains outside its scope. A payment-file total matching the payroll total is useful, but does not establish that the underlying pay rules or employee inputs were correct.

<ArticleTable caption="Five payroll accountability questions for Finance leaders">
<thead><tr><th scope="col">Question to practise</th><th scope="col">Evidence to examine</th><th scope="col">When the answer is unclear</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><th scope="row">1. What am I being asked to approve?</th><td>Defined decision, scope and applicable delegation</td><td>Clarify the approval instead of signing a broad assurance statement unsupported by the review</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">2. Are differences explained?</th><td>Reconciliation with investigated exceptions and unresolved items visible</td><td>Assign investigation or escalate; do not hide differences within an aggregate total</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">3. Are adjustments traceable?</th><td>Reason, calculation reference, prior processing check and approval</td><td>Request evidence of the adjustment's origin and treatment</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">4. Does the report distinguish findings from estimates?</th><td>Population, period, limitations and separately described employee impacts</td><td>Request a clearer assessment before relying on the number</td></tr>
<tr><th scope="row">5. Are resources available to resolve the cause?</th><td>Prioritised action, dependency, accountable owner and funding decision</td><td>Bring the blocked decision to the authorised sponsor</td></tr>
</tbody>
</ArticleTable>

For executive reporting, use the [payroll board-reporting guide](/blog/payroll-in-the-boardroom). Keep technical validation with appropriately skilled reviewers and define how their findings inform your approval.

## Ask five questions at every difficult handoff

Use these prompts when an issue moves between Payroll, HR, Finance, Operations or Technology:

1. What exact decision or action is needed from the receiving team?
2. Which facts are established, and which assumptions remain unresolved?
3. What employee group, pay period or process may be affected?
4. Who has authority to decide, and what is the escalation route if they cannot act?
5. How will we confirm the agreed action happened and had the intended result?

Record the answer and next review date in the issue or decision record. For the wider ownership arrangements, see the [payroll governance pillar](/blog/payroll-governance).

## Practise three decisions before the next payroll issue

These fictional scenarios are discussion exercises, not client results or legal determinations. Ask participants to explain their answer before reading the suggested response.

### Scenario 1: the change is approved, but the facts are incomplete

An HR manager sends an approved role change without an effective date. The pay-run cutoff is approaching. Should Payroll infer the date from the email, or should HR obtain the missing information through the agreed route?

**Suggested response:** HR obtains a confirmed effective date and any other required facts from the authorised source. Payroll explains the processing dependency and potential timing impact. If a decision cannot be made within the normal route, escalate it promptly; incomplete information should not quietly become an assumed rule.

**Why:** approval and completeness are different checks. Practise producing a usable instruction and identifying who can decide the exception.

### Scenario 2: the total reconciles, but adjustments remain unexplained

Finance receives a payment reconciliation that balances overall. Two retrospective adjustments have no reason recorded. Does the matching total establish that the adjustments are valid?

**Suggested response:** request the adjustment evidence, including the reason and prior processing check. Record the unresolved items and follow the organisation's approval and escalation arrangements. Avoid an unsupported blanket instruction either to release every item or withhold all employee pay.

**Why:** a total can reconcile while individual transactions remain wrong. Practise distinguishing the financial reconciliation from the evidence supporting each adjustment.

### Scenario 3: the employees were paid, so the issue is marked closed

The historical correction is complete, but Operations still sends late inputs and the permanent interface fix has no owner. Should the overall finding be closed?

**Suggested response:** separate the completed employee correction from the unresolved process issue. Identify the owner, current containment and required resources, then agree what evidence will support closure of the permanent fix.

**Why:** a payment outcome does not demonstrate prevention. Practise stating the decision the sponsor needs to make rather than simply recirculating the issue log.

## Convert the discussion into specific learning actions

Finish with one action for each unresolved priority. State the task to improve, the barrier, the responsible owner and the evidence you will examine at follow-up. Some actions will involve learning; others will require better templates, clearer delegations, access or a system change.

For example, a targeted joint session might practise preparing a complete employment-change instruction and reviewing its payroll handoff. The manager then observes a later example and checks whether the revised process was used. Attendance alone does not demonstrate that improvement.
